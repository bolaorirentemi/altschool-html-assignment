My Story

From Electrical Engineering to Software Engineering

My journey into technology did not begin with a computer science degree. It began with Electrical and Electronics Engineering.

During my engineering education, I developed an interest in automation, electrical systems, control systems, and understanding how different components can work together to achieve a useful result.

I also gained practical exposure to areas such as electrical installation, industrial automation, motors, and control systems. These experiences taught me that solving a problem is not only about knowing the theory. It is also about understanding the problem, testing ideas, learning from mistakes, and finding a solution that works in the real world.

That way of thinking naturally led me towards software engineering. I became interested in the possibility of creating digital products that can solve problems for many people at once.

Software engineering therefore feels less like abandoning my engineering background and more like adding another tool to it. I want to combine engineering thinking with software development and artificial intelligence to create practical solutions.

My Learning Journey

Every developer starts somewhere, and for me, this is the beginning of a new chapter.

I started by learning HTML and understanding how webpages are structured. I am also learning CSS and JavaScript as I gradually develop the skills needed to create interactive and functional websites.

As I continue, I expect to explore frontend development, backend development, databases, APIs, Git, GitHub, artificial intelligence, and deployment.

I know that the journey will not always be easy. There will be confusing concepts, bugs that seem difficult to understand, and moments when progress feels slow.

However, my engineering background has taught me that difficult problems become easier when they are approached patiently and consistently.

I am not trying to know everything at once. I am focused on understanding one concept, practising it, and using it to build something useful.

My goal is simple: to become better with every project I build and every problem I solve.
